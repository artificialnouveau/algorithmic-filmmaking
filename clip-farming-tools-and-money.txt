 CLIP FARMING: TOOLS AND HOW PEOPLE MAKE MONEY
 Research notes, compiled June 2026
============================================================================

WHAT "CLIP FARMING" MEANS
-------------------------
Clipping = taking short, self-contained moments out of long-form content
(a 3-hour Twitch stream, a podcast, a gaming VOD, a webinar) and reposting
them as standalone short-form videos on TikTok, YouTube Shorts, Instagram
Reels, etc.

"Clip farming" is the high-volume, often automated version: running it like
a content mill. Many accounts, many clips per day, optimized purely for the
algorithm and for paid-per-view programs. The "farming" framing (like crop
farming or "engagement farming") is what makes it interesting as subject
matter - it is content production reduced to extraction + volume.

Note: the term is used two ways.
  1. Streaming/creator economy sense - the money-making practice below.
  2. Casual sense - "farming clips" = harvesting good moments from streams.
This document is about sense 1.


============================================================================
 THE TOOLS
============================================================================

1) AI AUTO-CLIPPING TOOLS (find the "viral" moment, cut, caption, format)
   These ingest a long video and auto-produce vertical clips with captions.
   - OpusClip (opus.pro)  - the dominant one; "virality score", auto-reframe
     to vertical, auto-captions, one-click publish to all platforms.
   - Submagic              - best-in-class animated / emoji / word-by-word
     captions (the TikTok-Reels caption look).
   - Reap                  - clipping + scheduling.
   - VEED, Descript        - more general editors with clipping features.
   - Clippie.ai, Vugola, Ssemble - newer entrants in the same space.
   What they automate: scene/highlight detection, vertical reframing (face
   tracking / active-speaker crop), auto-captioning, thumbnailing, posting.

2) STREAM-SOURCING / CLIPPER PLATFORMS (get the raw footage, post, get paid)
   - Clipt        - download raw streamer footage, clip it, post to short-form,
     get paid on performance.
   - Sx Bot / Clipify (Discord bots) - clipping workflows for streamer
     communities.
   - Whop          - marketplace where "clipping campaigns" live; no follower
     / watch-hour minimums, which is why beginners start there.

3) "CLIPPING CAMPAIGN" / PAID-PER-VIEW MARKETPLACES
   Brands, creators, and labels post campaigns; clippers claim them, produce
   clips, post to their own accounts, and get paid per verified view.
   - Whop, ClipAffiliates, and similar campaign marketplaces.

4) THE FACELESS-CHANNEL STACK (run it like a factory)
   - Bulk scheduling / multi-account posting tools.
   - AI voiceover + stock/gameplay background for fully synthetic clips.
   - Spreadsheet/automation glue to manage many niche accounts at once.
   The whole point: high-volume publishing across TikTok + Shorts + Reels
   (+ Pinterest) without ever showing a face, because the platforms reward
   volume.


============================================================================
 HOW THE MONEY ACTUALLY WORKS
============================================================================
Successful clippers STACK several income streams on the SAME clip:

A) BRAND / CREATOR CLIPPING CAMPAIGNS  (the main money)
   - Campaigns pay a CPM: roughly $1-$5 per 1,000 verified views.
   - You join a campaign, clip the supplied content, post to your accounts,
     get paid on the views you generate.

B) PLATFORM CREATOR FUNDS  (stacks on top, lower rate)
   - TikTok Creativity Program, YouTube Shorts monetization, Instagram
     bonuses: roughly $0.02-$0.05 per 1,000 views.
   - Small per view, but it pays out on the SAME clips as the campaign.

C) DIRECT DEALS WITH STREAMERS / PODCASTERS
   - A streamer or podcaster hires you to run their clips channel.
   - Flat retainer ($200-$2,000 / month) OR revenue share on the clips.

D) AFFILIATE / PRODUCT  (advanced)
   - Drive clip traffic to affiliate links, your own products, or other
     channels you monetize.

Illustrative figure from the reporting: a teenager reportedly turned a $200
laptop into ~$10,000 in two months by cutting Twitch streams into 60-second
clips and posting them to Whop campaigns paying $1-$5 per 1,000 views.
(Treat headline numbers as marketing-flavored; the MECHANISM is the real
takeaway, not the jackpot.)


============================================================================
 THE PIPELINE (what a clip farm actually does, step by step)
============================================================================
   1. SOURCE   - pick streams/podcasts/VODs with clip-worthy moments.
   2. HARVEST  - pull the raw footage (download or capture).
   3. DETECT   - find the "good" 30-60s moments (AI highlight detection,
                 or manual).
   4. CUT      - trim to a self-contained beat.
   5. REFRAME  - crop 16:9 -> 9:16, track the active speaker / face.
   6. CAPTION  - burn in animated word-by-word subtitles.
   7. PACKAGE  - hook in the first 1-2s, title, thumbnail.
   8. DISTRIBUTE - post across many accounts/platforms on a schedule.
   9. MONETIZE - collect on campaign CPM + platform funds + retainers.
  10. OPTIMIZE - double down on what the algorithm rewarded; kill the rest.


============================================================================
 RELEVANCE TO THE ART ANGLE ("clip farming through live coding")
============================================================================
Steps 2-7 of that pipeline are EXACTLY what the Scene Ripper project already
does (scene detection, shot classification, object/face detection, OCR,
color/brightness analysis, render). The commercial clip-farm stack is a
black box optimized for engagement; an art piece can expose or subvert it:
  - Make the FARMING LOGIC the visible subject (live-code the selection rules
    on stage, Depoorter-style but performative).
  - Farm by the "wrong" criteria on purpose (every dead-air pause, every
    blank frame, every logo) to satirize engagement optimization.
  - Run the same auto-clip pipeline but route it to nonsense / refusal
    instead of virality.

The crowded reference points (so you know the room): Christian Marclay (The
Clock), Natalie Bookchin (Mass Ornament), Dries Depoorter (Flemish Scrollers,
The Follower). The under-worked seam is doing this LIVE and content-aware via
live coding.
